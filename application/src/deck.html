Summertime

Commit together. Settle together.

Private, all-or-nothing settlement for importers who share shipping containers, built on Canton Network with USDCx.

November 2026github.com/linoxbt/summertime
The problem

Small importers share containers,
and carry all the risk of doing so.

Ada imports phone accessories from Guangzhou. She needs 4 CBM in a 60 CBM container. She pays the consolidator first and hopes the container fills, while the importer next to her in Computer Village ships through the same consolidator.

How groupage works today

  • Importers who cannot fill a container share one to cut freight cost per unit.
  • Each pays the consolidator upfront, coordinated over WhatsApp.
  • Nobody can verify the container is really filling, or that space is really reserved.
  • Competitors learn each other's volumes through the consolidator's group chats.
Sources: Wigmore Trading on China–Nigeria consolidation; Shipping Position, "Cargo consolidators exploiting Nigerians with arbitrary charges"; ThisDay (2021), "Nigeria's ravenous ports". Ada is an illustrative persona.

Why it stays broken

Four coordination failures, one root cause: trust.

01

Pay-first risk

Importers pay before the container is confirmed full. If it doesn't fill, their money is stuck with the consolidator.

02

First-mover reluctance

Nobody wants to commit first, so containers fill slowly and ship late.

03

Competitive leakage

Importers in the same market learn each other's volumes and suppliers through the shared shipment.

04

Partial execution

Some pay, some don't. The container ships late, or not at all, and refunds are slow and disputed.

Summertime3
The solution

Money moves only if the container fills.

1

Forwarder opens a campaign

60 CBM container, a fixed rate per CBM, a deadline, and a failure bond of 1–5% of the container's value.

2

Importers reserve privately

Each importer reserves its CBM and locks USDCx against a forwarder-signed reservation. The money stays in the importer's own allocation.

3

Container fills: one atomic settlement

Every importer's payment settles to the forwarder in a single Canton transaction. All or nothing.

4

Container doesn't fill: funds released, bonus paid

Every importer gets its funds back and claims a share of the forwarder's bond. Committing early is rewarded, not punished.

Worked example (from the specification)

60 CBM at 150 USDCx/CBM · 200 USDCx bond

Six importers reserve 60 CBM → 9,000 USDCx settles to the forwarder in one transaction; the bond is returned.

Five importers reserve 54 CBM → nobody pays; all funds released; the 200 USDCx bond is split pro rata (51.85, 44.44, 40.74, 33.33, 29.63 USDCx).

Both cases run today as automated tests against a reference CIP-56 token.

Privacy and trust

Competitors stay invisible to each other.

"Competing importers cannot see each other's reservations, volumes or identities. Your forwarder sees your reservation, exactly as it does today, and so does Summertime, which enforces the settlement rules. Funds move only if the container fills."

PartyCanCannot
ImporterSee only its own reservation, receipts and rounded progressSee any other importer's identity, volume or payment
ForwarderSee every reservation; choose when to settle once fullSettle below the threshold or after the deadline; overbook; avoid paying the bond
Summertime (operator)Co-sign campaigns; hold the bond; pay bonusesMove importer funds; settle on its own

We state the trust model plainly: the forwarder and Summertime see reservations, and Summertime holds the bond in custody until a neutral escrow replaces it (Phase 2).

Summertime5
Why Canton

Only Canton offers all three properties together.

Privacy

Sub-transaction privacy

Each importer sees only its own leg of a settlement, even inside one shared transaction. On a public chain, every competitor's volume would be visible.

Atomic

Multi-party settlement

Six independent importers pay one forwarder in a single transaction. If any leg fails, nothing moves.

CIP-56

Token-standard allocations

Importers lock funds in their own allocation, not a pooled escrow. USDCx gives businesses a dollar asset they understand.

Summertime depends only on the Canton token-standard interfaces, so the same contracts run with USDCx or Canton Coin.

Product and architecture

A Daml commitment engine with a simple app on top.

Importer app

Mobile-first: reserve CBM, see USDCx, sign, track progress, claim bonus.

→

API and worker

Node.js / TypeScript; deterministic command IDs; offset-tracked automation.

→

Daml contracts

Campaigns, reservations, commitments, bond, bonuses (summertime-core).

→

Canton + USDCx

Validator, Global Synchronizer, USDCx registry (CIP-56).

Settlement guard

Campaigns are co-signed by the forwarder and Summertime. A commitment can only be settled from inside the campaign's settle step, which checks the threshold and deadline on-ledger.

Enforceable bonus

Each importer's own commitment makes it eligible for the bond. The forwarder cannot skip the bond lock, leave importers out, or cancel a bonus without settling that importer's funds.

Market

First market: groupage containers, China to Lagos.

Buyer

Independent freight forwarders and consolidators running groupage containers on China–Lagos routes. They want faster fills, credible commitments and fewer payment disputes.

User

SME importers shipping roughly 1–15 CBM per shipment. They want their money safe, their volumes private, and a fair rate.

Ideal first customer

A forwarder running at least two groupage containers a month, with an importer base it already knows by name (hypothesis to validate).

Why this market first: groupage is how most Nigerian SMEs import, and many of Nigeria's large importers started there. Consolidator practices are a public complaint. Sizing: we have not found a sourced figure for Lagos groupage volume, so we will build the estimate from interview data (containers per month, CBM per importer) rather than publish a guess.

Sources: Wigmore Trading; ThisDay (2021); Shipping Position.
